The mimic this lane had to survive

Pleistocene Lake Lahontan tufa marks a shoreline, not a fault. The highstand is about 1332–1338 m (USGS Circular 1267; GSA Bulletin 110:1318, Adams and Wesnousky 1998, 1338.5 m in the Jessup embayment). Tufa and silica-cemented sand were excluded from the seeds before fitting. Of the emitted dots, 1.77% fall in the 1320–1345 m band on the dequantized 3DEP-derived 100 m mean elevation. That diagnostic does not rescue the null result, and it does not show the shoreline mimic took over the map.

The other mimic is a line of water wells along a road. Alignments made only of wells were rejected unless one point had a geothermometer of at least 120 °C, or the neighbourhood contained a spring, a sinter/travertine, a 2 m anomaly, or a vent. 4,591 wells were eligible as supporters. 200 of them were anchors. 1,588 springs were used.
